BAND: deep sine sub-bass, punchy kick, sharp claps on 2 and 4, tight 16th hats, one crash. Muted electric piano or plucked synth for chords. NO guitars, NO strings, NO orchestral swell, NO risers other than the one specified.

ARRANGEMENT — follow these times exactly:

The voice lands ONE syllable on each: "light-er, LIGHT-ER". Pump, pump, PUMP, PUMP.
4.0–4.5s: TOTAL SILENCE. Nothing. Do not fill this half-beat; the gap is what makes the drop land.
4.5s: THE DROP. Crash, four-on-the-floor kick, claps on 2 and 4, 16th hats, sub-bass on A. Full energy, and it does not let up.
6.0 / 7.25 / 8.5s: three quick accents — an open hat or snap on each, on top of the groove, not instead of it. Each marks a physical flick.
11.0s / 17.5s / 23.0s: the bass root moves (A to F to C to G), each marked by a crash and a bass stab. The groove never breaks across these — the drums run straight through.
27.5s: drums STOP dead — not a fade, a stop. Sub-bass and voice only, level eased back about 45%. The contrast is the effect.

MELODY — make it stick: give every couplet a common pop contour, first line rising in pitch, second line falling, the shape of Twinkle Twinkle. Keep the tune simple and stepwise so it is singable after one listen. The exception is the hook: "light-er, LIGHT-ER" repeats the same two syllables with a clear upward LEAP the second time, while the bass underneath moves DOWN. That contrary motion is the signature — it must be the most distinctive interval in the track. The word "lighter" is both the first hook at 3.3s and the last word at 32s, closing the loop.

FEEL: a modern app commercial — not a club track, not a jingle. Warm, assured, forward-moving.

DO NOT INCLUDE: no count-in, no spoken intro or outro, no crowd or applause, no vinyl crackle, no siren or air-horn, no key change, no half-time breakdown, no second vocalist, no rap section.

MIX: bass-forward and punchy but not loud. Dry vocal with only a short slap delay. Leave midrange space — this plays under motion graphics with no narration and every word must stay intelligible. Nothing should clip; keep the four stabs as the loudest low-end moments in the track.

## C. WHY THIS SHOULD BE ADDICTIVE

Built on Jakubowski et al.'s earworm study (3,000 participants), which found
involuntary musical imagery is predicted by three things. Each is used here:

1. **Faster tempo.** 120 BPM sits in the earworm range.
2. **A common global melodic contour** — the shapes already familiar from
   Western pop, e.g. first phrase rises, second falls, as in *Twinkle Twinkle*.
   Instruction: **make every couplet rise on the first line and fall on the
   second.** "Swipe it left, let it go" rises; "right for the ones you wanna
   know" falls.
3. **An unusual interval or repetition** that marks it out — the study cites the
   *Smoke on the Water* riff and the *Bad Romance* chorus. Here that is the
   **"light-er, LIGHT-ER" figure**: the same two syllables repeated, the second
   time a clear leap higher, over four descending bass stabs. Contrary motion —
   voice up while the bass goes down — is the distinctive interval.

Reinforcements:
- **The hook returns.** "Lighter" is the last word of the song as well as the
  first hook, so the 32 seconds close the loop.
- **The hook arrives at 3.3s**, well inside the first few seconds.
- **Tight rhyme throughout**: go/know, clear/disappear, down/around, save/wave,
  room/bloom, hand/stand. Every couplet closes.

## E. THE ENDING

The endcard is 4.5 seconds and must not feel like the song was cut off.

- Drums stop **completely** at 27.5s — not a fade, a stop. The contrast is the effect.
- Sub bass and voice continue, softer.
- The tagline lands in the clear, then the final word **"Lighter"** — the hook
  returning as a resolution.
- **Stop dead on the downbeat at 32.0s.** No fade-out, no reverb tail, no
  instrumental outro. The film cuts to black on that frame.

## F. LYRIC CONSTRAINTS — check any regenerated lyrics against this

SwipeClean is live on Google Play, so a sung claim is held to the same bar as
the store listing.

**Never sing:**
- "nothing ever leaves your phone" / "we send nothing" — false; the free tier
  links AdMob and RevenueCat, which transmit device and advertising identifiers.
  *"Cleaning done right where you stand"* is the true form: the analysis is local.
- "no quality loss" / "lossless" — the approved wording is "without visibly
  losing quality". *"Nothing you'd ever miss"* is the sung form.
- "free" attached to Smart Clean, Convert, video compression or Compress All —
  all Pro.
- "AI" / "machine learning" — the detectors are MD5, perceptual dHash and
  variance-of-Laplacian.
- Superlatives — "#1", "best", "fastest" — Play policy.
- Competitor or OS-vendor names.
- iPhone or App Store references — Android only.

## G. IF THE RENDER IS WRONG

- **Too long / too short** — it must be 32.000s. Trimming afterwards breaks the
  ending; regenerate with the length restated.
- **Stabs not escalating** — restate: "four bass hits an eighth apart, each
  louder and lower, the fourth twice the weight of the first."
- **No silence before the drop** — the 0.5s gap at 4.0–4.5s is essential.
- **Fades out at the end** — restate "stop dead on the downbeat, no fade".
